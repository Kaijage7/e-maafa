Production-scale primitives are partial: notification delivery has an in-process `@Async` executor of core 2, max 6, queue 200 (`AsyncConfig.java:16-26`); public/login/upload rate limiting is useful but in-memory/per-instance by design (`FixedWindowRateLimiter.java:7-15`); management exposes only health/info (`application.yml:79-86`); frontend freshness is polling-heavy (topbar 45s, public landing 60s, response/EOCC dashboard 30s, executive watch 30s), and earlier F29 evidence still tracks zero SSE/WebSocket. A fresh scan found no Kafka/Rabbit/Redis/shared cache/Caffeine `@Cacheable`/SSE/WebSocket runtime. This means the stack can scale as a hardened modular monolith, but not yet as a crash-resistant national surge platform or AI/GIS workload platform without explicit event contracts, read models, shared throttling/cache, worker queues, observability, and load tests.
- Fix hint: Create a system-linkage control plane before microservice extraction or AI launch. Minimum: component dependency matrix (module owner, routes, APIs, tables, permissions, row scope, upstream/downstream dependencies), OpenAPI/event contracts, a real transactional outbox with consumers/replay/retry/dead-letter/correlation ids, shared rate limiting/cache where multi-node deployment is expected, async workers for notification fan-out/PDF/GIS/AI jobs, read-model projections for dashboards and public counters, SLOs and load tests for district/region/national/surge profiles, and AI feature-snapshot/model-run/prediction-event/human-disposition tables. Keep incident command, stock movement, finance, and approval workflows in the monolith until idempotency and audit timelines are proven; extract notification/integration and analytics read models first.
